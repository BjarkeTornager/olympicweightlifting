# Olympic weightlifting knowledge, fuelling and video review

Reviewed 10 September 2026. This extends the personal lifting brief and four-week evidence workspace. The aim is a useful, verifiable coaching loop: understand the athlete, inspect relevant evidence, choose one practical adjustment, then review its effect.

These sources provide educational principles, not validation of this app. Public links and original short summaries are used; no paid programs, full articles or training datasets were copied. The app lists the source type, publisher and review date. Sources do not endorse the product. The source tool is curated content, not a live web search.

An older IWF nutrition news article was also considered. Its broad calorie and timing prescriptions were not adopted as personal targets. A federation-hosted article is not automatically stronger evidence than a relevant consensus or position stand.

## Implemented experience

In **Train → Lifting coach**:

- **Review lifting video** opens the private video preparation flow.
- **Plan food around training** drafts a request for practical before/after-session suggestions using existing records and preferences.
- **Learn from trusted sources** groups technique/video, programming and nutrition reading into expandable sections.

Coach has a read-only `lifting_knowledge` tool with `technique`, `programming` and `nutrition` topics. It supplies source links, a review date and focused guidance only when relevant. It does not grant permission to change records. Personal advice still requires the relevant private journal reads.

Nutrition guidance connects food preferences, upcoming session timing and demands with practical meal options. It distinguishes complete and partial food days, avoids inferring energy balance from missing logs, and preserves reviewed changes to diet targets. Meal suggestions are never recorded as food already eaten. Individual competition weight management and medical nutrition belong with an appropriately qualified professional.

## Video review: what it actually does

1. Choose a browser-decodable local video up to 100 MB and two minutes. H.264 MP4 is the most portable starting point; unsupported codecs produce an actionable error.
2. Choose 0.5–6 seconds and the lift. Load, a question and the training date provide context. Review the clean and jerk separately when a whole attempt exceeds the interval limit.
3. The browser decodes **24 evenly sampled positions**. A shorter interval places the samples closer together. Nothing is uploaded during this preview.
4. Four JPEG sheets contain six frames each, with numbered source-time labels, in reading order. Whole frames are letterboxed, not cropped. The maximum stored sheet dimension is 1920 pixels; ordinary photo uploads keep the existing 1280-pixel limit.
5. **Save frames & add to message** stores the four sheets under the signed-in account, directly in **Activity**, tagged `weightlifting` and `video-frames`. It does not run image classification. Successful partial uploads and stable upload IDs are reused on retry.
6. The existing Coach draft is preserved and the review request is appended. The user sends it through the normal message queue. The configured image-capable provider receives the sheets using existing private image handling and existing no-training/zero-data-retention routing filters.
7. Coach is instructed to check visibility and coverage, distinguish visible observations from hypotheses, refer to supporting timestamps, and give one cue or appropriate drill plus a next-attempt check when supported. It should decline an assessment when the relevant phase is missing or unreadable.

The original clip and audio are **not uploaded or stored**. They must be kept by the user for later playback or a new selection. Saved sheets can be enlarged, retrieved, downloaded and deleted through the existing private image library. The catalogue has group identifiers and per-sheet source-time ranges in its labels; the model sees timestamps in the pixels. There is no new persistent video object, calibrated motion data or structured biomechanical measurement table.

During preparation the dialog shows a compact player and progress view. This keeps the real video visible while sampling: Linux WebKit can suspend seeks when a paused player is scrolled out of view. The form remains in place for retry, and errors clear the preparation status. Browser tests verify the player's visibility during seeks as well as changing decoded pixels.

A cancelled/interrupted upload can leave some saved sheets in Activity; the UI and privacy page explain this. Removing a draft attachment does not delete a saved sheet. Clearing/deleting image data is separate from conversation text. Uploads retain owner checks, account pinning, quotas, request size bounds, metadata stripping and private no-store responses. No public media URL is created. The content security policy allows local blob video playback without permitting arbitrary external video providers.

## Limits and the next quality step

This is **experimental video-derived visual feedback**, not validated motion analysis. Requested browser seek times are labels, not exact measured capture times; decoders can return neighbouring frames. Even 24 samples can miss a rapid event. A single camera, occlusion, blur and downsampling constrain what is visible. The app does not calculate exact joint angles, bar speed, forces, a complete bar path, competition decisions, injury probability or a technique score. It does not listen to the clip or inspect every frame. No new model-quality score is claimed.

[OpenRouter supports native video inputs for compatible models](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/overview/multimodal/videos). [Google's video documentation](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video-understanding) describes video understanding and sampling options; its guidance warns that default sampling can miss rapid motion. A native-video model is worth benchmarking, but provider compatibility, sampling controls, cost and retention guarantees must be verified together. We did not switch models or weaken privacy filters for this implementation.

[MediaPipe Pose Landmarker](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker) detects 33 approximate body landmarks. That alone does not locate a barbell or validate weightlifting measurements. [OpenCap's filming guidance](https://www.opencap.ai/best-practices) discusses camera configuration and occlusion. These are candidate technologies for a later measurement pipeline, not evidence that a monocular gym clip yields trustworthy biomechanics automatically.

Recommended next work, not shipped:

- Build a consented evaluation set of varied snatch, clean and jerk clips, including different angles, lighting, experience levels and body proportions. Include successful lifts, common issues, ambiguous clips and non-lifting controls.
- Have qualified weightlifting coaches independently annotate visible evidence, the most useful priority, uncertainty and an acceptable drill/check. Resolve disagreement explicitly.
- Compare the current frame approach with native-video models using blind ratings for useful priority selection, unsupported claims, timestamp accuracy, appropriate abstention, latency and cost. Keep a held-out set rather than optimising against every test clip.
- Add private original-video storage, resumable uploads, deletion/export and durable processing if repeated playback, side-by-side comparisons or closed-tab jobs justify it.
- Add pose/bar tracking only with confidence/occlusion handling and validation against suitable reference measurements. Show uncertain or unavailable measurements rather than invented precision.
- Track whether a recommended cue actually helped at the next session, with user feedback and source-linked comparisons. Existing agreed plans already support a manual version of this loop.

## Verification

Deterministic tests cover interval bounds, chronological sampling, video-sheet encoding and metadata stripping, automatic-classification exclusion, owner isolation, retries, deletion and read-only tool behavior. Browser checks use a generated two-second moving-colour H.264 clip, including pixel changes across frames, four-sheet preview, partial-upload retry, preserving drafts and explicit submission. The synthetic clip contains no person and no health data. Model responses are stubbed; no private athlete media or paid model calls are used for evaluation.

The fixture `tests/fixtures/lifting-motion.mp4` is generated from coloured backgrounds and a moving white rectangle with macOS AVAssetWriter. It is original test data, not third-party coaching footage. The production build and checks passed: 23 progression tests, 128 domain/database/protocol tests with no skips, and all 252 browser tests across Chromium, WebKit and Firefox. These verify software behavior, not the accuracy of model-generated coaching. Hosted verification is recorded with the release.
